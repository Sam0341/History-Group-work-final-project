Background

Origins of the Dispute

The roots of the Belize and Guatemala territorial dispute trace back to the 1859 Treaty of Boundaries between Britain and Guatemala. Guatemala has long argued that unmet treaty obligations, particularly a promised road to the Caribbean render the treaty void, giving Guatemala historic rights over the land.

Belize gained independence in 1981, but Guatemala did not formally recognise its sovereignty for over a decade. Even after recognition in 1991, territorial claims persisted. In 2019, both nations agreed to submit the dispute to the International Court of Justice (ICJ).

"The territory Guatemala claimed amounts to roughly half of Belize's total land area a dispute that touches the very identity of a young nation."

Chronology

Key Dates

Twelve pivotal moments from the 1859 Treaty to the 2019 ICJ submission.

1859

Treaty of Boundaries

Britain and Guatemala sign the 1859 Treaty, defining the borders of British Honduras. Guatemala later argues that Article 7's road building obligation was never fulfilled, rendering the treaty void.

1862

Crown Colony Status

British Honduras becomes an official Crown Colony of the British Empire, cementing British governance and deepening Guatemala's resentment over unfulfilled treaty terms.

1931

Sarstoon River Border

An exchange of notes between Britain and Guatemala reaffirms the southern boundary along the Sarstoon River but tensions simmer as Guatemala insists on its broader claim.

1945

Guatemala's Constitutional Claim

Guatemala enshrines the claim to Belize in its new constitution, declaring British Honduras to be Guatemalan territory. This marks the formal start of a state level territorial dispute.

1965

Webster Proposals

US mediator Bethuel Webster proposes a settlement giving Guatemala significant control over Belize. Belizeans decisively reject the proposals, galvanising their push for full independence.

1975

UN General Assembly Support

The United Nations General Assembly passes resolutions calling for Belize's right to self determination, independence, and territorial integrity, a major diplomatic victory for Belizean leaders.

1981

Independence of Belize

Belize officially gains independence on September 21, 1981. Guatemala refuses to recognise the new state, and Britain deploys troops to protect the new nation's sovereignty against potential aggression.

1991

Guatemala Recognises Belize

A decade after independence, Guatemala formally recognises Belize as a sovereign state. However, recognition does not mean abandonment. Guatemala continues to press its territorial claim through diplomatic channels.

2000

OAS Facilitation Begins

The Organisation of American States (OAS) begins formally mediating the dispute. Both countries enter a period of confidence building measures and negotiated frameworks to manage tensions along the border.

2008

Special Agreement Signed

Belize and Guatemala sign a Special Agreement to submit their territorial, insular, and maritime dispute to the International Court of Justice but only once both populations approve it in referendums.

2019

ICJ Referendums & Submission

Both Belize and Guatemala hold historic referendums. Majorities vote in favour of ICJ arbitration. The case is formally submitted to The Hague, where it now awaits a binding international ruling on the century old dispute.

Ongoing

ICJ Proceedings Continue

Both nations file memorials, counter-memorials, and legal arguments with the ICJ. The outcome will shape the borders of Central America and the lives of Belizeans for generations to come.

Research

Annotated Bibliography

Primary and secondary sources consulted for this project.

Primary Source · Treaty

Treaty of Boundaries, 1859

Britain & Guatemala · Convention of April 30, 1859

The foundational document of the dispute. This treaty defined the boundary between British Honduras and Guatemala. Article 7, which obligated Britain to help build a cart road, became the crux of Guatemala's later argument that the treaty was void due to nonperformance.

View Treaty Text (PDF)
Secondary Source · Book

Shoman, Assad. Thirteen Chapters of a History of Belize

Angelus Press, Belize City, 1994

A seminal text written from a Belizean perspective. Shoman provides a comprehensive account of colonial history, the independence movement, and the territorial dispute, offering essential context for understanding Belizean national identity in the face of external claims.

View on Open Library
Secondary Source · Book

Dobson, Narda. A History of Belize

Longman Caribbean, London, 1973

One of the earliest comprehensive histories of Belize written for a Caribbean audience. Dobson's work contextualises the 1859 treaty within broader colonial power dynamics, demonstrating how European rivalries directly shaped the present day dispute.

View on Internet Archive
Primary Source · Legal

ICJ Special Agreement, 2008

International Court of Justice · December 8, 2008

The legal framework agreed upon by Belize and Guatemala to submit their dispute to the ICJ. This document outlines the precise questions the Court is asked to resolve, and the procedural conditions including public referendums required before submission could occur.

View ICJ Case Page
Secondary Source · Report

OAS Facilitation Reports 2000–2018

Organisation of American States · Various years

A series of annual OAS reports documenting confidence building measures, border incidents, and negotiation progress between Belize and Guatemala. Invaluable for understanding the diplomatic dimension of the dispute over nearly two decades of facilitated dialogue.

View OAS Process Page
Secondary Source · Book

Bolland, O. Nigel. Colonialism and Resistance in Belize

SPEAR, Belize City, 1988

Bolland examines the social and political resistance of the Belizean people against both colonial rule and Guatemalan territorial pressure. This work is essential for understanding how ordinary Belizeans experienced and pushed back against the existential threat of territorial absorption.
